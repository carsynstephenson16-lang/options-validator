## (a) Executive logic — the honest causal map

A quarterly print is the aggregation of ~90 days of operating reality. Public signals can precede it only through four causal channels, each with a different clock:

1. **People who already know the quarter** (insiders) leak information through legally-mandated disclosure. Constraint: insider-trading blackout policies close the trading window roughly 2 weeks before quarter-end through 1–2 days after the release, so the informed insider trade lives at **T-45 to T-15 relative to the print, not T-14 to T-0** (see §1). Publication lag: 2 business days (Form 4).
2. **Counterparties whose own disclosures reveal the quarter's inputs** (suppliers, customers, contract awarders). TSMC's monthly revenue (~10th of month, cross-filed as SEC 6-K) mechanically precedes NVDA/AMD/AVGO prints by ~5–7 weeks; hyperscaler capex guides precede power-name demand narratives; DoD announces awards >$9M same-day at 5 p.m. ET. These are the cleanest true *leads* in this survey — public, dated, structured. But the mapping from counterparty disclosure to the subject's print is a modeling assumption, not an identity.
3. **The market's own conditional pricing** (options). The chain prices the *magnitude* of the coming move well (implied move ≈ ATM straddle; realized lands inside the 1σ implied move ~60–68% of the time for single names) and prices *direction* weakly — the directional-predictability literature (IV spread, skew, O/S ratio) is real but small, earnings-conditional, and documented to decay post-publication.
4. **Slow fundamental exhaust** (hiring, job postings, WARN, H-1B LCA, congressional trades, alt-data panels). These correlate with revenue *trajectory* on a quarters-to-a-year horizon; almost none of them resolve to "this specific print beats/misses." Congressional trading is structurally disqualified for print-timing by the 45-day STOCK Act disclosure ceiling.

**The honest hierarchy for "seeing the print":** channel 2 (counterparty disclosures) has the best lead-time-to-noise ratio; channel 1 (insiders) has the best academic evidence but an awkward clock; channel 3 (options) is free to this platform but the most crowded; channel 4 is background texture, not print-timing.

**The universal trap:** point-in-time reconstructibility. EDGAR filings are immutable once accepted (acceptance timestamp = knowable-at anchor). Almost everything else restates: USASpending backfills for years [VERIFIED-DIRECT: a Palantir USG award with Base Obligation Date 2025-11-21 carries Last Modified Date 2026-03-24 — the record changed four months after the obligation]; I/B/E/S rewrites history (Ljungqvist et al.); job postings vanish when filled; consensus pages are snapshot-only. A backtest that pulls "historical" data today is look-ahead-biased unless the source is immutable or self-snapshotted.

## (b) Signal classes

### 1. Insider transactions (SEC Form 4)

**Source + API (all free, keyless; declared User-Agent with email required):**
- Submissions API: `https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json` (10-digit zero-padded). [VERIFIED-DIRECT 2026-07-16: PLTR CIK 0001321655 returns 504 Form 4s in the recent-filings window.]
- Full-text search JSON backend: `https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=...&forms=4&startdt=YYYY-MM-DD&enddt=YYYY-MM-DD` (also `ciks=`). [VERIFIED-DIRECT: endpoint live, returns Elasticsearch-style JSON.]
- Daily index (the point-in-time backbone): `https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/daily-index/YYYY/QTRn/form.YYYYMMDD.idx` — one file per filing day, rows keyed by form type and **filing date**. [VERIFIED-DIRECT: `form.20260715.idx` contains Form 4 rows dated 20260715.]
- Payload: Form 4 XML (`ownershipDocument` schema, mandatory since 2003-06-30) — `transactionCode` (P = open-market purchase, S = sale, A = grant, M = exercise), shares, price, post-transaction holdings, `isDirector`/`isOfficer`/`officerTitle` flags. [SOURCED: SEC ownership XML spec, https://www.sec.gov/info/edgar/ownershipxmlspec-v1-r1.doc]

**Cost:** free. Paid wrappers (sec-api.io, Form4API, EODHD) save parsing labor only; pricing UNVERIFIED and unnecessary here.

**Lag:** statutory 2 business days after transaction execution (Sarbanes-Oxley §403, effective 2002-08-29; pre-SOX it was up to ~40 days). [SOURCED: Fenwick, https://assets.fenwick.com/legacy/FenwickDocuments/Corp_Sec_08-29-02.pdf; Olshan, https://www.olshanlaw.com/newsroom/alerts/Section-16-Reporting-Requirements-as-Amended-by-the-Sarbanes-Oxley-Act] EDGAR FTS indexes new filings in under ~60 seconds and covers 2001+ (SOURCED but second-hand — the sub-researcher could not fetch the SEC FTS FAQ directly, HTTP 403; facts are from the search summary of https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/efts-faq.html). [VERIFIED-DIRECT corroboration: an FTS phrase query bounded to 2001 returned 0 hits, consistent with index start mid-2001.]

**Point-in-time reconstructibility: STRONG — the best in this survey.** Filings are immutable; acceptance datetime is recorded; daily-index files let you rebuild exactly the set of Form 4s public on any date back to ~2001 (clean XML from mid-2003). One hazard: Form 4/A amendments restate earlier transactions — a rigorous engine must key on the *original* 4's acceptance datetime, never the amendment's (Inference, but mechanically obvious).

**Predictive value (academic):**
- Lakonishok & Lee 2001 (RFS 14:79–111): predictive power almost entirely from **purchases** (sales ≈ noise), concentrated in **small firms**. [SOURCED: https://www.lsvasset.com/pdf/research-papers/Insider-Trades-Informative.pdf]
- Jeng, Metrick & Zeckhauser 2003 (REStat 85:453–471): insider purchases ≈ **6%+/yr abnormal**; ~¼ of it accrues within 5 days, ~½ within the first month — fast decay. [SOURCED: https://direct.mit.edu/rest/article/85/2/453/57400/]
- Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012 (JF 67:1009–1043) — the key paper: **opportunistic** (non-calendar-routine) insider trades earn ~82 bps/month VW and **predict firm-specific news and earnings-announcement returns**; routine trades ≈ zero. [SOURCED: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2012.01740.x]

**The blackout-timing problem (critical, addressed honestly):** company insider-trading policies typically close the window ~2–3 weeks before quarter-end and reopen 1–2 trading days after the release — survey data: ~45% of policies close at ~2 weeks before quarter-end, 76% reopen within 1–2 days post-earnings. [SOURCED: Wilson Sonsini SV150 report, https://www.wsgr.com/a/web/bPTkLm9NChAxLS7DiWkjfv/wilsonsonsini_insider_trading_report_20251111.pdf; Orrick, https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/06/Insider-Trading-Policy-Key-Terms-and-Trends; Harvard corpgov, https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2021/06/02/determinants-of-insider-trading-windows/] **Consequence (Inference, well-grounded): the informed pre-print insider buy lives at roughly T-45 to T-15, in the open window between prints — NOT in the two weeks before the release.** A signal design that scans T-14..T-0 will find only 10b5-1 plan executions (pre-scheduled, low-information) and anomalies. The model must look one full open-window back and accept JMZ's fast decay as a headwind.

**Evidence grade: B+** (robust, replicated, but the strong versions are small-cap-concentrated and the effect is strongest for purchases only). **Crowding: B** — the naive version (OpenInsider et al.) is fully commoditized; the opportunistic-vs-routine decomposition plus blackout-aware timing is genuinely more effortful and less crowded.

### 2. Congressional / political trading (STOCK Act)

**Sources:** Senate eFD (https://efdsearch.senate.gov — no API, terms-acceptance cookie gate, HTML+PDF); House Clerk (https://disclosures-clerk.house.gov — no official API but a clean bulk path: `public_disc/financial-pdfs/<YEAR>FD.zip` containing an XML index; individual PTR PDFs at `public_disc/ptr-pdfs/<YEAR>/<DocID>.pdf`; scanned/handwritten filings need OCR). [SOURCED: CRS R47818, https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R47818.html] Free aggregators: CapitolTrades (free), Quiver Quantitative (free views, paid API), Unusual Whales (paid, specifics UNVERIFIED), Senate/House Stock Watcher (free community JSON; parsing quality UNVERIFIED). Reliability risk concentrates in ticker mapping and the fact that disclosures report dollar **ranges** ($1,001–$15,000 …), not sizes. [SOURCED: Senate Ethics PTR guidance, https://www.ethics.senate.gov/public/?a=Files.Serve&File_id=312473C0-B6CA-44C9-A68F-C65FC5F0D8E9]

**Cost:** free (gov + CapitolTrades); paid only for structured convenience.

**Lag — the disqualifying fact:** PTRs are due ≤30 days from *notice* of the transaction, hard ceiling **45 days after the transaction**; penalty for lateness is a $200 fine with essentially zero enforcement. [SOURCED: Senate Ethics guidance above; https://www.govgreed.com/learn/stock-act-explained] **A trade disclosed up to 45 days after execution cannot lead a specific quarterly print** — for most quarters the disclosure lands *after* the print the trade preceded. (Inference, but arithmetic.)

**Point-in-time:** adequate on the *disclosure* axis (key strictly off filing/disclosure date, data back to 2012); severe look-ahead bias if keyed off transaction date — which much published "congress alpha" content quietly does (Inference, echoed by the NBER/Belmont methodology critiques).

**Predictive value — both sides:** Pro (older): Ziobrowski et al. 2004 (JFQA) ~1%/month Senate outperformance [SOURCED: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A39406479940758D59E09FDCB8EE9BEC/S0022109000003161a.pdf/]; Ziobrowski 2011 House ~6%/yr. Null (newer, post-STOCK-Act, methodologically stricter): Eggers & Hainmueller 2013 (portfolio-level, no outperformance); NBER w26975 "As Feckless as the Rest of Us" (no skill) [SOURCED: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26975/w26975.pdf]; Belmont et al. 2022 (random stock picking; House buys underperform ~26 bps); J. Public Economics 2022 [SOURCED: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0047272722000044]. Contested residual: Hanousek et al. 2023 finds leadership-subset alpha even from disclosure dates [SOURCED via https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/political-power-and-profitable-trades-us-congress]. Weight of evidence: null-to-weak for the broad feed.

**Evidence grade: D** (for pre-earnings use specifically; C- as a slow thematic tilt). **Crowding: D** — the most retail-saturated alt-data feed in existence (copy-trade ETFs, viral trackers), and structurally too slow anyway.

**Cost:** all free.

**Lag — two-track:** the *announcement* track is fast (DoD same-day 5 p.m.; company 8-K within 4 business days if material). The *database* track is slow: agencies report to FPDS within ~30 days, DoD data lags ~90 days, so USASpending trails reality by 30–90 days. [SOURCED: CRS R44027, https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R44027]

**Point-in-time reconstructibility: POOR for USASpending, GOOD for announcements.** FPDS has no correction cut-off — agencies retroactively add/modify/delete actions years later. [VERIFIED-DIRECT: Palantir USG award, Base Obligation Date 2025-11-21, Last Modified Date 2026-03-24 — a four-month-later restatement, live proof that today's pull ≠ what was knowable then.] Honest reconstruction requires the dated monthly archive/delta files or your own captures. DoD daily announcement pages and EDGAR filings are immutable and dated — use those as the point-in-time anchors (Inference).

**Predictive value:** award-announcement event studies find positive but small abnormal returns for winners, scaling with contract size relative to firm assets [SOURCED: https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/20-05.pdf]; longer-run, government contracting is associated with *lower* valuations and eroding ROA gains (Esqueda, Ngo & Susnjara) [SOURCED: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426619301505]. **The award→revenue lag is the killer for print-timing:** revenue recognizes under ASC 606 over the performance period, quarters-to-years after award. PLTR is the canonical trap — funded Remaining Deal Value (~$11.2B) vs unfunded IDIQ ceilings (~$12.3B); a "$10B ceiling" headline can convert to ~zero next-quarter revenue (figures Tier-2/3 — Motley Fool https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/03/18/palantir-has-an-112-billion-revenue-backlog-a-10-b/ — must be rebound to PLTR 10-Q RPO disclosure before any production use). USAR: awards can be thesis-moving because they are large relative to a small cap, but revenue waits on plant ramp — UNVERIFIED HIGH-IMPACT, no specific USAR award pulled this session. VST/CEG: PPAs and capacity-auction results are mostly NOT in USASpending (commercial/ISO events) — 8-K Item 1.01 and DOE press releases are the right channel; no clean structured feed exists (DATA GAP).

**Evidence grade: C** (real but small announcement effect; weak print-timing linkage). **Crowding: C+/B-** — headlines arbitraged intraday by scraper vendors; residual edge only in the tedious ceiling-vs-obligation-vs-RPO reconciliation nobody does.

[SOURCED: https://www.aila.org/library/oflc-releases-public-disclosure-data-and-selected-program-statistics-for-q1-of-fy26] **Point-in-time: EXCELLENT** (immutable dated government files back to ~FY2008) — its one genuine virtue. Sign-ambiguous (layoffs = margin discipline or demand collapse). No earnings-surprise study found (DATA GAP). Grade: **C- evidence / C+ crowding.**

### 6. Options-market signals (direction, not magnitude)

Platform already caches full chains with NBBO/OI daily — zero marginal data cost, fully point-in-time **by construction going forward** (OI is next-morning data; volume same-day — do not mix the two clocks; Inference, mechanical).

**Literature, honestly summarized:**
- **Volume/put-call:** Pan & Poteshman 2006 (RFS 19:871–908): buyer-initiated open-position put-call ratios predict next-day (>40 bps) and next-week (>1%) cross-sectional returns — BUT built on proprietary CBOE open-buy classification that standard OPRA/NBBO feeds cannot reproduce; volume/ΔOI heuristics are a weaker approximation. [SOURCED: https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/19/3/871/1646711]
- **O/S ratio:** Johnson & So 2012 (JFE 106:262–286): options-to-stock volume ratio predicts firm-specific earnings news; low-O/S beats high-O/S ~0.34%/week; mechanism = short-sale costs pushing informed bears into options. [SOURCED: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X12000797]
- **Skew/smirk:** Xing, Zhang & Zhao 2010 (JFQA 45:641–662): steepest-smirk stocks underperform by ~10.9%/yr risk-adjusted and have the worst next-quarter earnings shocks — an explicit skew→surprise-direction link. [SOURCED: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1107464] Van Buskirk: skew predicts crashes **only in short-window earnings periods** — earnings-conditional. [SOURCED: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228204703_Volatility_Skew_Earnings_Announcements_and_the_Predictability_of_Crashes]
- **IV spread (put-call parity deviations):** Cremers & Weinbaum 2010 (JFQA 45:335–367): positive call-put IV spread predicts outperformance ~50 bps/week — and the paper itself documents the effect **weakening within its own sample**. Pre-earnings IV spread amplifies/predicts announcement reactions (JBF 2017, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426617300791). [SOURCED: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=968237]
- **Magnitude (where options are actually good):** realized move lands inside the straddle-implied 1σ move ~60–68% of the time for single names — IV systematically overstates realized (variance risk premium); regime-unstable (ORATS reports long-straddle profitability in the 2026 season: https://orats.com/blog/earnings-straddles-strong-season-2026). [SOURCED: https://resonanzcapital.com/insights/options-straddles-and-earnings-move-estimates]

**Honest bottom line:** direction predictability is statistically real in 1990s–2000s samples, small, earnings-conditional, decaying, and built partly on data granularity (signed open-position volume) this platform does not have. **Evidence grade: B- for direction (A- for magnitude). Crowding: D — the most-arbitraged bucket in this survey; these papers are canonical and in every factor library.**

### 7. Analyst revision momentum + peer read-throughs

**Revision momentum evidence:** PEAD (Bernard & Thomas 1989 — 25–50% of the price response drifts over ~60 trading days post-announcement); Chan, Jegadeesh & Lakonishok 1996 (JF 51:1681–1713 — earnings and price momentum survive mutual controls); Gleason & Lee 2003 (TAR 78:193–225 — post-revision drift stronger for high-innovation revisions, **weaker for high-coverage firms**). [SOURCED: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=370425] Zacks Rank is the practitioner codification (vendor self-published — not independent validation; https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf). **Decay caveat: the residual anomaly concentrates in low-coverage names — this book's mega-caps (NVDA/MSFT/AMZN) are the worst place to expect it** (Inference from Gleason-Lee + Menzly-Ozbas coverage results).

**The point-in-time consensus problem — the crux, and it is genuinely bad:** Ljungqvist, Malloy & Marston 2009 "Rewriting History" (JF): across seven I/B/E/S downloads 2000–2007, **1.6%–21.7% of matched observations changed between downloads**, nonrandomly, materially altering backtest results. [SOURCED: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01484.x] Even the paid gold standard is not PIT without vintaged snapshots. Free sources (Yahoo `query1.finance.yahoo.com` unofficial JSON, Zacks free tier, stockanalysis.com, Nasdaq) are **snapshot-only — no revision history**; Wayback coverage of JS-rendered estimate pages is unreliable (UNVERIFIED as a dataset); Koyfin's historical estimates are paid. [SOURCED: https://data.nasdaq.com/databases/ZREV; https://www.koyfin.com/help/release-notes/v3-81-historical-estimates-actuals-surprises/] **Free PIT consensus history does not exist off the shelf. The only clean free path is forward snapshot collection starting now** — which this owner's equity-research repo already does in miniature (`ticker_market_pull.py` dated captures).

**Peer read-throughs — the most tractable lead in this survey:**
- Economic-link predictability: Cohen & Frazzini 2008 (JF 63:1977–2011) "customer momentum" — supplier returns predictable from named-customer shocks, attributed to limited attention [SOURCED: http://www.econ.yale.edu/~shiller/behfin/2006-04/cohen-frazzini.pdf]; Menzly & Ozbas 2010 (JF 65:1555–1580) — cross-predictability declines with coverage/institutional ownership. [SOURCED: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=989080]
- **TSMC monthly revenue:** Taiwan rule = monthly revenue by the 10th of the following month; TSMC cross-files a **SEC 6-K** (immutable, point-in-time clean). Verified examples: March-2026 revenue filed 2026-04-10 (accession 000104617926000136, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000104617926000136/tsm-revenue20260410.htm); March-2025 filed 2025-04-10; Aug-2025 filed 2025-09-10. Calendar: https://investor.tsmc.com/english/monthly-revenue/2026. **This leads NVDA/AMD/AVGO prints by ~5–7 weeks, free, structured, on a known calendar.** Caveats: total-company revenue blends AI/HPC with phones/auto (decomposition = assumption); timing can slip (June-2026 report reportedly postponed off a typhoon day — UNVERIFIED exact reissue date).
- **Hyperscaler capex guides → NVDA/AVGO demand and VST/CEG power-demand narrative.** Directionally well-sourced; magnitude mapping to a specific print is Inference, not a quantified elasticity. All specific capex figures circulating are Tier-2/3 and must be rebound to each hyperscaler's 8-K/10-Q before production use.

**Evidence grade: B+ (read-throughs) / B (revision momentum, but D-for-mega-caps). Crowding: B/B+ for read-throughs (sell-side does this, but it needs domain mapping — exactly the limited-attention gap Cohen-Frazzini documents); D for large-cap revision momentum.**

## (d) What could be built here — 3 candidates for THIS platform

Selection logic: the platform already has (i) EDGAR tooling and (ii) daily-cached option chains with NBBO/OI. The best candidates minimize new data plumbing, maximize point-in-time integrity, and have surviving evidence. No thresholds below — those are owner-typed at registration per repo doctrine.

### Candidate 1 — Blackout-aware opportunistic insider-cluster signal (Class 1)

**Sketch.** Ingest Form 4 XML for the universe via the submissions API + daily-index (both verified live). Classify each insider as routine/opportunistic per Cohen-Malloy-Pomorski's calendar-pattern method (an insider who trades in the same calendar month in ≥N prior years = routine; classification parameters are owner-typed). The event is an opportunistic open-market **purchase cluster** (multiple distinct insiders, dollar-size-weighted) landing in the open trading window — measured relative to the *next* confirmed earnings date, i.e., in the T-45..T-15 band, never T-14..T-0. Pre-registration test: define the event at Form 4 **acceptance datetime** (point-in-time honest), paper-trade a pre-declared option structure into the next print, score against the platform's existing loss-gated ledger. Hypothesis is registered before any historical peek; the historical panel (EDGAR is fully reconstructible to 2003+) serves as the backtest, with the forward paper window as confirmation. Sample-size reality: on a 14-name universe, opportunistic purchase clusters are RARE (mega-cap insiders mostly sell) — the honest design probably requires widening the universe or accepting a multi-year accrual window, and that should be confronted at registration, not discovered after.

### Candidate 2 — TSMC 6-K monthly-revenue read-through to NVDA/AMD/AVGO prints (Class 7b)

**Sketch.** The platform's EDGAR tooling pulls TSMC's monthly-revenue 6-K the day it files (~10th of each month, verified accessions above). Construct a point-in-time monthly series (each observation timestamped by EDGAR acceptance — immutable). The registered question: does the intra-quarter trajectory of TSMC monthly revenue (the two or three months mapping into a fabless customer's fiscal quarter, mapping owner-specified) carry information about the customer's print direction or about the sign of the post-earnings move, over and above what the option chain already implies at T-1? The option-chain cache provides the control (implied move, skew at T-1) — making this a *joint* test of "public supplier data not yet in the option surface." Paper-trade a pre-declared structure only when supplier signal and pre-registered mapping agree; score on the existing ledger. Everything is free, dated, and immutable; the fiscal-calendar mapping (TSMC calendar months → NVDA fiscal quarter with its ~1-month offset) is the main specification risk and must be frozen at registration.

### Candidate 3 — Pre-earnings IV-spread / skew direction study on the platform's own chain cache (Class 6)

**Sketch.** Zero new data. From each day's cached chain, compute the Cremers-Weinbaum call-put IV spread and the Xing-Zhang-Zhao smirk (OTM-put IV minus ATM-call IV) for each universe name, daily, into each print. Registered question: conditional on the pre-earnings window, does the sign/steepening of these measures predict the sign of the post-print excess move? This is a *replication-first* design — the honest framing is "do canonical, probably-decayed signals still carry anything in this universe post-2020?" — where a null result is a successful outcome (per operating doctrine). The platform's PIT integrity is perfect going forward (own snapshots); the historical depth is bounded by how far back the chain cache goes. Key pre-registration disciplines: OI is next-morning data (never same-day), volume is same-day; the Pan-Poteshman signed-open-volume construction is NOT reproducible from NBBO/OI — register only the reproducible variants.

## (e) How this could be lying — the strongest case against each candidate

**Candidate 1 (insider clusters).**
1. *Survivorship of the literature, not the firms:* the headline insider-alpha results are 1975–2001 samples, small-cap-concentrated, and pre-date the 2002 two-day rule that made the signal universally visible — visibility is exactly what arbitrages a signal away. Post-2002 net-of-crowding evidence in mega-caps is far weaker (Inference; LL 2001 explicitly finds the effect concentrated in small firms).
2. *Already-priced:* Form 4s hit free trackers within minutes; JMZ shows half the abnormal return accrues within a month of the *trade* — much of it likely within days of the *filing* now. What's left by the time a T-45 cluster reaches a T+0 print may be zero.
3. *Selection on rarity:* on 14 mega/mid-cap names, opportunistic purchase clusters may occur a handful of times a year. Any backtest "significance" on that sample is noise-mining by construction; the design must pre-commit to the widened universe or to multi-year patience.
4. *The blackout logic can invert:* a purchase at T-40 reflects the insider's view of the *previous* quarter's aftermath and long-horizon value, not necessarily the upcoming print — the causal link from "insider bought 6 weeks ago" to "this print beats" is the weakest link in the chain (Inference).

**Candidate 2 (TSMC read-through).**
1. *Most-watched read-through on Earth:* TSMC monthly revenue moves NVDA/AMD within minutes of release; sell-side publishes the read-through note the same morning. The claim that anything survives to the customer's print 5–7 weeks later contradicts the same efficient-pricing logic the option-chain control is meant to test — and Cohen-Frazzini's limited-attention mechanism is weakest precisely for the most-covered stocks in the market (Menzly-Ozbas: predictability declines with coverage).
2. *Composition noise:* TSMC total revenue blends AI/HPC, smartphone seasonality, and auto; a hot iPhone-build month can masquerade as an AI demand signal. The decomposition assumption is unfalsifiable at monthly granularity.
3. *Regime dependence:* in a capacity-constrained AI regime, TSMC revenue is supply-determined, not demand-revealing — it may say nothing about whether NVDA *beats consensus*, which is a game against expectations, not against physical shipments. Consensus itself already ingests TSMC data (analysts read the same 6-Ks), so the "surprise" may be orthogonal to the signal by construction.
4. *Small-n:* three fabless customers × 4 prints/yr = 12 events/yr. Years to significance; every early "result" is a story, not evidence.

**Candidate 3 (options direction).**
1. *Published-and-decayed:* Cremers-Weinbaum documented weakening within its own 1996–2005 sample; twenty years and one options-volume explosion later, the prior should be near-zero for mega-caps with penny-wide, MM-dominated surfaces. The likeliest outcome is a clean null — acceptable per doctrine, but do not mistake it for a strategy.
2. *Construction gap:* the strongest published version (Pan-Poteshman) uses signed open-buy volume the platform cannot see. NBBO/OI approximations are a different, weaker signal wearing the same name — a positive result could be an artifact of the approximation, a negative result says nothing about the original.
3. *Mechanical confounds masquerading as information:* pre-earnings skew steepening is partly hedging demand and dealer inventory, not directional information; in heavily-covered AI names, skew moves can reflect structured-product flows (covered-call ETFs on NVDA, etc.) that are wholly non-informational. Any "signal" may be flow, and flow reverses.
4. *In-sample seduction:* with daily surfaces and many constructible measures (spread, smirk, term slope, ΔOI...), the garden of forking paths is enormous. Only strict pre-registration of ONE measure and ONE conditional window keeps this honest — the platform's discipline is the defense, and it must be applied before any historical look.

**Cross-cutting lie-detector:** all three candidates share one master risk — **the option surface at T-1 already contains every public signal in this report.** The registered null hypothesis for each should be phrased as "adds nothing beyond the chain's own implied distribution," because that is the world's default state and the burden of proof sits entirely on the signal.
